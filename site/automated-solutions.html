Case study · Cash

From month-end cash anxiety to a cash model the CEO can use

A UK-headquartered, multi-country services group funded by invoice finance. Leadership felt a worsening month-end squeeze but couldn't pin down why.

What was built

One workbook measuring total available cash (bank plus undrawn facility), not just the bank balance. It includes a month-to-month cash bridge with a plain-English explanation, reconciled debtor, unbilled and creditor movements, key-customer tracking, a 15-month projection with editable assumptions, a dashboard ranking the ten levers that move cash most, and a how-to guide for the CEO.

How AI was used

AI read and reconciled around 60 source files line by line (management accounts, consolidations, aged debtors and creditors, accruals and bank reports), then built the model, ran scenarios and drafted the CEO summary. CFO challenge caught its mistakes, such as a misleading supplier-payment conclusion that vanished once accruals were included. The AI in turn caught errors in existing reports, including a stale data feed and a reversed FX sign.

Case study · Margin

A margin improvement model for a mid-market services business

A multi-country services provider, much of it subcontracted work, running at a thin EBITDA margin under cash pressure.

What was built

A fully formula-driven model linked to the management accounts. It splits the P&L into four margin layers, analyses gross margin across nine service lines, sizes ten margin levers at conservative, target and stretch levels, and ends in an EBITDA bridge and a phased action plan with owners and KPIs.

How AI was used

AI reconciled the management accounts, budget, cash model and bank data, found the diagnostic patterns, sized each lever from actual cost lines, and built and checked the workbook: 756 formulas, zero errors, EBITDA tied to the management accounts to the pound. The judgement calls stayed with the CFO.

Case study · Process

Accounts payable review for an international services group

Two ledger companies, UK and US, three invoice streams and around 630 supplier invoices a month, with a stretched AP team.

  • Mapped every step across four departments, from inbox to payment run, with swim lanes for each invoice stream.
  • Found 11 handoffs, only 2 controlled by the system. Delays clustered at the uncontrolled ones.
  • Showed that invoices were invisible until keyed into the ledger, so ageing, accruals and time-to-pay could not be measured. One fix closes both the reporting gap and the control gap.
  • Built a volume and value baseline from seven months of ledger data, to measure improvement against.
  • Identified where AI is already saving time, such as complex telecoms allocations, and where automation should go next.
